COMMON QUESTIONS

Limits, in writing.

Are these official warnings?

No. Follow IMD, CPCB, NDMA and your local authority. Call 112 in an emergency. The plain labels on a card (warm, hot, unhealthy) are readings of the source number. They are not IMD heatwave declarations and not CPCB categories.

Why is there no 0–100 risk index?

A single index would hide which hazard moved, and it would need a population layer joined to the point. This page does not load population, so it does not invent a score. You see the source numbers instead.

Why can the air number differ from the Indian AQI?

The card is US AQI from Open-Meteo's air model. India's CPCB scale uses different bands. The model is coarse, about 45 km over India, so a street can differ from the cell.

Where is the machine-learning forecast?

Not on this page. A separate heat-only research model (a simple logistic regression) was compared with baselines on 60 of about 735 districts, using past days rather than a weather forecast. On days a heat spell starts, that model caught 19% one day ahead, 11% two days ahead, and 25% three days ahead. Those figures are onset recall on the 2024 test, not an overall accuracy. Because onset detection is weak, this page shows the source forecast instead of that model's probability.

What is not loaded, and why?

Fires (NASA FIRMS) and station air (OpenAQ) need a free key. This page is keyless, so those layers are absent rather than filled with example dots. Earthquake occurrence is not forecast.

METHOD

What the search actually does.

  1. Place: your text is sent to the Open-Meteo geocoding service with countryCode IN. Results are kept for 10 minutes in this browser tab only.
  2. Weather: the forecast endpoint, forecast_days=3, timezone=auto. The page shows the current model temperature, each day's high, low and precipitation, the highest high, and the precipitation sum. A missing day is reported. It is not replaced with zero.
  3. Air: the air-quality endpoint, current us_aqi and pm2_5. If either is missing, that line says unavailable.
  4. Quakes: the USGS FDSN event endpoint, format=geojson, minmagnitude=3, maxradiuskm=300, starttime=30 days ago, ordered by magnitude, limit 20.
  5. The locator drawing is computed in the browser from a rough outline. It is not loaded from a boundary file. City squares are a fixed list of approximate coordinates.

Know the reading. Know what to do.

What this page can show for each hazard, and ordinary precautions. Official instructions override this page.

01HEATTemperature

What we show: the model's highest temperature over three daily values, and each day. Not an IMD heatwave declaration.

Stay safe: drink water often, avoid hard outdoor work from noon to 4 pm, and check on older neighbours.

02RAINRainfall

What we show: total model precipitation over those three days. Not a flood warning.

Stay safe: do not walk or drive through flood water, move valuables up, and keep a charged phone and a torch.

03AIRUS AQI

What we show: current US AQI and PM2.5 from a coarse model. Not a CPCB station on your street.

Stay safe: on bad days keep windows closed, limit outdoor exercise, and use an N95 mask outside.

04QUAKESRecent quakes

What we show: recent quakes of M3 or more within 300 km. We do not predict quakes.

Stay safe: Drop, Cover and Hold On. Stay away from windows. After shaking stops, check for gas leaks.

05FIREFires

What we show: nothing yet. A keyless page does not draw fake fire detections.

Stay safe: follow local authorities, keep away from smoke, and move early if told to evacuate.

In an emergency, call 112.

Hazard data that anyone can read.

BhuNetra means "earth's eye". It is a free, open-source research page that puts public hazard data in plain language. It is not a government service.

PRINCIPLES

Real data. Plain words.

Real data only

Every live number comes from a source named on the Data page. A failed request stays blank.

Honest limits

The heat research model is weak on the first day of a spell, so its probability is not shown as if it were a warning.

Open to all

No account, no API key, and no sign-up. Hindi and English carry the same facts.

Still outside this page.

  1. Fire detections and ground air stations. Both need a key, so they are not pretended here.
  2. A population-weighted risk index. Not shown until a real population layer is joined in the open.
  3. The heat model's own 24–72 hour probabilities. Held back because onset recall is 11–25%, as written on the Data page.

These are gaps, not hidden features.
